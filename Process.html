API Data-Retrieval Flow with AI / ML Layer

The customer is unknown at checkout, and the customer type is not assumed. After one external data fetch, the router first asks whether the applicant is a seller with a Sổ Bán Hàng account (Track C). If not, it asks whether a salary was entered and whether CIC returns a history: salaried with CIC is Track A, salaried without CIC is Track B. CIC is read as a feature of the applicant, not as the thing that decides the segment. With no seller account and no salary, the application fails. No person sits in the decision path: every case ends in approve, decline or another payment method, within seconds. Thirteen AI / ML applications sit in the right-hand lane, with the focus on step 4a and on the fallback step in Tracks A and B.

Focus 1: inside step 4a

Step 4a is where the fetched data becomes something a model can decide on, so most of the speed and cost gain starts here. The customer always enters the salary and employer. AI does not fill them in; it only runs a brief check that the entry matches the fetched data.

Focus 2: the fallback step in Tracks A and B

Today this step asks for a payslip or labour contract, then an employer HR check, then manual review. For new customers with no CIC, BHXH, tax or Open Banking data this is the main path, not an exception. Document AI (AI 7) changes it to a fully automatic step:

  1. Classify the upload and extract employer, wage and pay period.
  2. Check for tampering: fonts, metadata and pasted regions. This is a signal, not proof: it misses photographed or AI-generated payslips.
  3. Cross-check against at least one source the customer did not supply: the BHXH wage (a floor, not true income) or salary inflows. If none came back in step 4, the customer can consent in the same session to a VssID signed extract or a bank link.
  4. If an independent source agrees, income is verified and the decision runs in seconds.
  5. If no independent source exists or the sources disagree, there is no HR check and no officer: the application is declined with a reason code, and the customer is offered other payment methods or CASA onboarding. Expect more declines here; AI 13 tracks them and appeals are handled afterwards.

AI / ML layer

Thirteen applications, numbered as in the diagram. Each one names the manual or slow step it removes. Examples are the companies’ own reported figures. Models that need default labels (AI 1, 8, 9, 10, 11) start as expert scorecards; see the model-development note below.

  1. AI 1

    Pre-scoring and capacity on file · Step 0, Track C fast lane

    A scheduled job forecasts each seller’s monthly inflows and scores default risk, then stores a limit with an expiry. At checkout the fast lane only checks that the limit still covers the order value.

    Removes: The Sổ Bán Hàng fetch and analysis at checkout.

    Example: Shopify Capital makes offers from sales history with no application; MYbank approves its 310 loan in under a second.

  2. AI 2

    Merchant and order risk score · Step 1

    Scores the order before any personal data is collected: amount, product category, the merchant’s past default and fraud rates, time of day. The score travels with the application to step 9.

    Removes: Manual merchant risk reviews.

    Example: Affirm scores every purchase individually.

  3. AI 3

    Liveness, deepfake check and retry router · Step 2c

    The NFC chip read is the anchor; face match and injection detection sit on top. A small model separates technical failures (guide a retry or switch to VNeID) from suspicious ones (stop).

    Removes: Branch referrals for technical failures.

    Example: WeBank uses facial recognition for remote KYC.

  4. AI 4

    AML alert scoring · Step 2d and the step 4 refresh

    Name matching tuned for Vietnamese names clears false matches using date of birth and CCCD. A second model ranks alerts and auto-closes low-risk ones with a logged reason. Likely sanctions hits are declined at once with a logged reason; Compliance reviews them afterwards, outside the checkout.

    Removes: Analyst time on false alerts.

    Example: HSBC reports 60% fewer false positives in transaction monitoring.

  5. AI 5

    Fraud graph model · Step 4

    Applicants, devices, phones, addresses and merchants become a network. The model flags dense clusters, such as many applicants on one device, and its flag is read in step 5. Families often share a phone, device or address, so the flag triggers automatic step-up checks (re-authentication, VNeID), never a decline on its own; a case that fails the checks is declined. Device data needs its own legal basis in the privacy notice (step 2).

    Removes: Manual investigation of fraud rings.

    Example: Grab uses a graph neural network that needs few labelled fraud cases.

  6. AI 6

    Income / Feature Engine models · Step 4a Focus

    Four models prepare the evidence: match entities, label transactions, build a reference income, flag conflicts and gaps. A brief match check then compares the salary and employer the customer entered in step 4b with that reference. The customer’s entry is never filled in or changed by AI. The BHXH wage is a floor: a gap against the declared salary is not proof of fraud. Credit Risk defines the “close” tolerance and which figure the decision uses (declared salary when match or close, otherwise the most conservative evidence). Entities are matched on fixed keys first (CCCD, enterprise tax code); fuzzy matching is only for company names when no key exists.

    Removes: Statement reading and manual comparison of the application with the evidence.

    Example: Tink sorts income into five categories and 21 sub-categories for lenders; Nubank learns features from raw transaction sequences instead of hand-built ones.

  7. AI 7

    Document AI · Fallback in Tracks A and B Focus

    Classifies the upload, extracts employer and wage, checks for tampering, and cross-checks against the salary the customer entered, the BHXH wage and salary inflows. A match with at least one independent source (BHXH wage or salary inflows) returns to the decision with no human step. Without an independent source the application is declined with a reason code and the customer is offered other payment methods; the employer HR check is removed.

    Removes: The employer HR check and manual document review.

    Example: MSB uses AI and RPA to classify and process files.

  8. AI 8

    Thin-file score · Track B

    A separate model for applicants with no CIC history, built on merchant purchase history and BHXH contribution months. Both can be empty (first-time buyers, informal workers), so the model must handle missing inputs. Telco data is left out of this version: it has no source in step 4, and would need its own lane, API and legal basis.

    Removes: Blanket declines and manual review of no-CIC applicants.

    Example: K Bank uses telecom bill and data-usage history.

  9. AI 9

    Low-and-grow order ceiling · Cold start, Tracks B and C

    Every loan still equals its order value. What grows is the largest order value a new customer can be approved for: small orders first, with the ceiling rising automatically after on-time repayments. A ceiling below the order value declines the whole order, so its effect on conversion is tracked (AI 13) and the starting ceiling is set with that in mind.

    Removes: Manual review of short histories.

    Example: Nubank models limit increases with survival analysis.

  10. AI 10

    Seller capacity analysis · Step 8a

    Reconciles Sổ Bán Hàng sales with tax data and CASA inflows where they exist, flags circular orders and sudden spikes, and forecasts monthly repayment capacity: revenue less costs, using inventory or supplier records or an industry margin assumption, with personal and business money separated. Cash sales cannot be verified, so they get a haircut. If Sổ Bán Hàng is mostly self-entered, independent reconciliation carries more weight.

    Removes: Manual reconciliation.

    Example: Mercado Pago analyses about 2,000 transactional variables.

  11. AI 11

    Credit scoring and decision engine · Step 9

    First checks that the loan amount equals the order value from step 1. Then credit-committee rules and one default-risk model per track answer a single question: can this customer repay that exact amount? It approves or declines, with no partial amount and no counter-offer. It first reads the customer’s open HLB exposure in real time, so parallel orders at several merchants cannot exceed capacity. Every decline carries reason codes, and the grey band is declined with reason codes and other payment methods; there is no manual queue.

    Removes: Per-case human approval.

    Example: Upstart reported 91% of loans fully automated in Q2 2026.

  12. AI 12

    GenAI explainer and appeal assistant · Step 9 output, after the decision

    Turns reason codes into a plain message for the customer and lists other payment methods at once. Drafts a summary for any later appeal or audit. It is not in the approval path and never changes the score.

    Removes: Manual case write-ups and decline letters.

    Recommendation; no published case cited.

  13. AI 13

    Model monitoring · After the decision

    Automatic checks on input drift, score distribution, approval rate by segment and first-payment defaults by merchant and track, and conversion by order ceiling, with a fallback to rules.

    Removes: Periodic manual model checks.

    Example: Nubank’s risk team tracks over 1,000 indicators weekly.

Human oversight: no person is in the decision path. People act only outside it: Compliance after a sanctions decline, appeals, sampled checks, and model approval and monitoring (AI 13). Vietnam’s AI Law 134/2025/QH15 and Decision 33/2026/QD-TTg (from 15 Aug 2026) list AI credit scoring and automated credit decisions without credit-officer approval as high-risk, with a finance-sector deadline of 1 Sep 2027. High-risk is not a ban, but the safeguards that come with it (explanation, appeal, human review on request, logging) must be confirmed with HLB Legal & Compliance. Checked against law-firm summaries, not the full text.

Model development: AI 1, 8, 9, 10 and 11 need default labels, but a new POS product has no repayment history, and declined applicants have no outcome (reject inference). The first release therefore uses an expert scorecard on the simulated dataset in the case, then runs champion / challenger once real labels exist. Assumptions to confirm: step 4 data may all return unknown for new customers; Open Banking needs the customer to sign in at another bank, so it is optional; a concurrent-loan check against HLB exposure runs in step 9; whether Sổ Bán Hàng entries are self-entered is not stated in this file and is treated as a risk. The no-human design accepts a higher decline rate where data is missing or the score is in the grey band; the legal position on fully automated decisions needs confirming.
